BUILDING A SALES TEAM
Source: Building Team Module

You cannot scale without a sales team. This is built from experience inside one of the largest solar sales teams in the world (50+ reps) - what they did right, what they did wrong, and how to do it better.

HIRING:

Always require a Loom video application. If they don't submit a Loom video, don't waste your time. Resume alone tells you nothing.

Process: Loom video → phone call → interview.

You can get the vibe on whether someone is an A player quickly. Ask specific questions that reveal:
- Do they actually want to be here?
- Are they hungry?
- Are they coachable?
- Are they willing to put in the work?

Frame the opportunity correctly on the onboard:
"If you want to make what a doctor makes, you have to study for 10 years. Here, if you're willing to put in the work right now, you can make that same money without any of that. But you have to be prepared to actually do the work."

Attitude is #1. You can train anyone up to be a good rep if they have the right attitude and access to the right material.

COMPENSATION - TIERED COMMISSIONS:

This is the single biggest lever for keeping and motivating A players. Most companies don't implement this. The companies that do see their sales team consistently push harder.

Structure: Tiered commission that resets weekly.

Example structure:
- Tier 1: First X deals/kilowatts/bookings → 10% commission
- Tier 2: Next X → 12% commission
- Tier 3: Above that → 14% commission

The metric can be: deals sold, kilowatts sold, dials made, bookings set - whatever makes sense for your business.

Why weekly resets: Even after a bad week, a new week = a fresh shot. They can always smash it. Monthly works too, but weekly keeps motivation consistently high.

Why tiers work: If a rep is at 90% of the next tier threshold, they'll push to 100% to unlock the higher rate. It's always pulling them forward.

If this was implemented at the $100M company, the top reps would have stayed. It's that important.

ONBOARDING:

1. Go through all relevant company details and materials
2. Complete this entire course - they must know it like the back of their hand
3. Watch recorded calls of top performers
4. Role play with sales manager
5. Run a supervised solo call
6. If eager and ready - give them a crack
7. If they underperform - audit the call, identify what to improve, coach up

The course is non-negotiable. Without it, they won't know the process, won't perform, and won't be an A player.

KPIs AND ACCOUNTABILITY:

Track everything weekly. Weekly sales meetings - keep them structured:
- Walk through the team numbers
- Highlight top performers
- Identify who underperformed and why
- Show one thing the whole team can do better
- Play a recording from a team member - show what they did well, how others can implement it

Weekly 1-on-1s with each sales rep:
- Ask them to submit a call from the past week where they felt stuck or lost
- Review it together
- Show specifically where and how it could have gone differently
- This is how reps improve 10x faster than just being left to figure it out alone

COMPETITIONS:

Constant competitions must be running. Leaderboards, prizes, recognition - whatever keeps the energy up. This is what makes reps feel valued and pushes them beyond their baseline.

SALES MANAGER:

Bring one in as soon as it's profitable to do so. Even at a couple million revenue, having a sales manager frees you up for revenue-generating activities.

Best option: Promote the top-performing rep on your team. They know the process, they've lived it, the team respects them.

Alternative: Hire externally. Same hiring principles apply.

The sales manager owns:
- Weekly meetings
- 1-on-1 coaching
- KPI tracking
- Culture and standards

CULTURE:

Set the standard and protect it. The culture you build determines who stays and who goes. A players want to be around other A players. They want to be pushed, recognised, and rewarded.

If you build this right, A players will stay. If you don't incentivise them, don't recognise them, and don't push them - they'll leave. And losing an A player is expensive.
