The 1-to-10 Calibration Question

A temperature check tool used near or at the close — after the customer has seen the full math. Do not deploy this earlier in the appointment; they don't have a real number to give until they've seen everything.

Purpose: Find out where they're at before attempting to close. Not a discovery tool — a calibration tool. It surfaces the real gap so you can address it specifically before going for the close.

The question:

"Okay guys, just to help me understand where you're at — on a scale of 1 to 10. One being this is the worst investment you've ever seen in your life, makes no sense, you're going to lose money on it. Ten being this is a great investment, it makes you money back, there's no risk, the ROI is good. Where are you guys at right now with how this is feeling?"

If they give a 7 or 8 (common response):

"Eight — that's not bad. What would make it a 10?"

Let them surface the actual concern. Then address whatever they said specifically — anchor back to the math, the guarantees, whatever resolves it.

Once resolved: "Cool. So now — feeling 10-ish?"

If they give a low number (4 or 5):

Do not push past it. Surface the gap directly: "Okay, that's lower than I'd expect — what's the main thing pulling that number down?" Handle the real objection first. Do not redeploy the 1-to-10 until you've actually moved them.

Tonality on "what would make it a 10?": Casual and curious, not loaded. You're genuinely interested in their answer, not setting up a technique.

What "10-ish" means: After handling their concern, "feeling 10-ish?" is a soft, low-pressure way to check they're now aligned. Casual tone — relaxed and assumptive, not a formal confirmation. If yes, move straight to the next step without adding extra pressure or another big check-in.
