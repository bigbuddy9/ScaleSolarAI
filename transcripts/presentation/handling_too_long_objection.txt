Handling "Just Get to the Price / This Is Taking Too Long"

IMPORTANT: These are principles, not a script to recite. Every rep's process and every customer is different. The example below shows HOW Jesse holds the frame in one real situation — do not copy it word for word. Extract the principle and adapt it to your own flow and the person in front of you.

When a prospect pushes to skip ahead and just get the price, do NOT jump to it. The core frame: jumping to the end would be doing them a disservice. You're informing them because this is a long-term, 25-year decision — and you can't make a decision like that properly without the full picture. Be respectful and cognizant of their time, offer to speed up, but hold the frame that this stuff matters.

THE FLOW:
1. Hold the frame — skipping to price would be a disservice to them
2. Check the time they were given for the appointment, so they're not caught out uninformed
3. Get into it — justify why the parts matter, then move forward

ONE REAL EXAMPLE — illustrative only, adapt it, don't recite it:

Prospect: "No, no, no, just get to the point. I have other things I have to do. How much do I have to pay you now?"

Jesse: "No, Ted, if I just jumped to the price and sent you a quote, I would be doing you a disservice, because we have to go through all of this stuff to make sure you're well informed. You wouldn't want to just jump into a decision and not make it properly. The easiest thing for me to do is just jump and send you the price — that's the easiest thing — but it would be doing you a disservice. It would actually not be in your best interest."

Jesse (checking the time): "They must have told you 90 minutes? They sort of said 60 to 90, because that's how long it takes. Do you have 60 to 90 today? Because if not, this stuff's very important, you should be well-informed. If you don't have that time free, we can reschedule for another time."

Prospect: "I think if you can speed this up, that'll be good." (Note: they won't always offer this — unique to this call.)

Jesse: "Of course, I can speed up parts of it, but there's certain parts we must go through as we're going to look at using some government funding, so you need to be well-informed. This is something that has the potential to save you $250,000 over the next 25 years, genuinely, and we guarantee that. You can't just jump into a decision like that, get the price, and think 'okay, yep, let's go.' You actually need to know who the company is that we're picking. Because if you don't know them and you're not confident in them, there's no point doing it. We're about to jump into the products, which I assume is what you want to see. So if you just bear with me for a couple minutes, we'll jump straight into the products, but this stuff is very important. Please don't get it twisted, or else we wouldn't go through it. Because we actually care about our customers — we care about making sure that you are well informed."

KEY MOVES:
1. Frame skipping as a DISSERVICE to them — not you wasting time, you protecting their decision
2. Anchor the stakes: 25-year decision, potential $250,000 savings, guaranteed
3. Be respectful and cognizant of their time — offer to speed up, but hold the frame
4. Check/lock the time expectation (60-90 min) and offer to reschedule rather than rush
5. Justify the parts you can't skip (government funding, knowing who the company is)
6. Tell them what's coming next (the products — what they want to see)
7. Ask them to bear with you for a couple minutes

Reversing the Order (Hard Cases):
Sometimes you read the room and know they won't engage until they see the price. Use critical thinking. You can reverse the order: show price first, walk them through savings, then go back and finish the presentation. Hard balance — figure out the type of person you're dealing with.

What NOT to do:
- Don't blindly skip to price without justification
- Don't ask them to evaluate price without context (price without savings = resistance)
- If you show price early, immediately follow with savings so they never sit on the number alone
