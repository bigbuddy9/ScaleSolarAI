PILLAR 3: Solar Industry Issues
Source: Presentation Modules

This is probably the most important pillar. Any price objection, "I want to look at other companies," "I want to get a couple other quotes" - it all comes back to how well you executed this pillar. You are pre-handling every objection that is going to come up at the end by educating them on what will happen if they do look around, so they already feel like they know the outcome and there is no need to do that.

Execute with full emotional conviction. You have to actually care and get genuinely worked up about this. If you do it half-heartedly, they'll say "mate, just jump through this." If you genuinely care, they'll be interested.

BEAT 1 - The Government Scheme Story (Pink Batts / equivalent):

Open with a question: "By the way guys, do you remember the [Pink Batts / equivalent scheme in your country]?"

Pink Batts = Australian government insulation scheme that went horribly wrong. Installers died, people tried to take advantage, it was unmanaged. Use an equivalent local example for UK/US markets.

Script:
"The thing is, once there's government funding involved, it gets ruthless. People lose basic human decency. They start cheating, scamming, doing whatever they can to get their hands on some government money. You've got one guy who's a plumber, aircon guy, even just a random tradie - he thinks, you know what, let me jump on this solar bandwagon, make a quick buck off the government. What these guys do - they just buy bulk products from China. All the racking, all the screws, all the equipment, dirt cheap obviously, then throw it straight up on your roof."

Key point: You would never know if you've got cheap stuff on your roof until years down the track. You're not going to climb up there on a blistering hot summer's day and burn your feet just to double check your panels.

BEAT 2 - Phoenixing:

"Guys, most importantly - because you can never 100% predict the future - if they do go out of business, do they have someone to back you up? A third party arrangement, insurance, guarantees that you're actually protected no matter what? Because we do. Or are they going to do what they all do - dump the business, shut down the phones, reset under a new business name, and run the whole scam over again. It's called phoenixing. You guys have probably heard about it by now, hey?"

Why say it that way: Either they know about it (yes, of course, it's so prevalent) or they haven't heard about it and think "far out, I should have heard about it by now - seems like it's such a common thing."

BEAT 3 - Price Difference Education:

"You can get a dirt cheap system - 5, 6, 10, 15 grand - how can they get it so cheap? Solar is a heap of components. Nowadays it's the panels, the inverter, the battery, the screws, the switches, the cabling, the racking, the conduit - so many different components. The only way you can ever go cheap is directly sacrificing the components that have gone on your roof. There's no other way to do it. And inevitably, cheap stuff doesn't last. You buy cheap, you pay the price later."

BEAT 4 - Price Transparency Pre-frame (Alex Hormozi principle - tell them it's expensive before showing price):

"Guys, I'm going to be very transparent with you - hands up, very open personally - we're not going to be the dirt cheapest out there in the industry. We never would be. To be honest, you don't actually want us to be dirt cheap. If we were dirt cheap, we wouldn't have been around for the past X years, we wouldn't plan to be here for the next 20, 25, 50 years, we wouldn't have all these guarantees and warranties put in place to protect you no matter what. And that's why I raised this at the start - just to make sure you pick a long-term company that genuinely cares about you guys. Makes sense?"

This is being transparent with them. They'll feel: he's being honest with us, we appreciate that. And when they see the price, they're already pre-handled.

BEAT 5 - The Fires (re-engage first):

"By the way, do you guys watch the news these days?"
[Build rapport off their answer, whatever it is]
"Are you familiar with the fires that have been happening from solar?"

Most say no - execute this regardless:
"One way they get the price that low, beyond just using the cheapest equipment, is using unlicensed installers. They'll have one electrician on the job but the rest are apprentices - two apprentices not using proper technique, just rushing the jobs. That's how they bring the cost right down. And that's why installations go by the wayside and aren't done properly."

Explain the mechanics: condensation gets into isolator switches. This makes you seem like an expert - you're well versed on a real problem. "That's your family's safety at stake guys. This could genuinely ruin people's lives."

Note: You can click through to a news clip but it tends to bore people. Achieve the same outcome through conversation and keep your connection with them.

BEAT 6 - Emotional Close of the Industry Issues Pillar:

Execute this with full sincerity. Get worked up. Actually care.

"If you knew how much it upsets me that there are companies out there with no integrity, companies who aren't there for you when things go wrong... It's a shame to me that I have to work in an industry that's actually been tarnished by these people who come in just to make a quick buck, and then they disappear. They leave families with all sorts of problems - fires, you name it. I've spoken to people personally who've been through this exact thing. They don't want solar at all anymore. They don't trust solar because of what happened to them."

Pause. Let them sit with it. Sincere. Very intentional tonality.

BEAT 7 - Cheap Solar Mistakes Slide:

Show visual examples of bad installations - burned panels, untidy installations, stuff left lying around.

"Look at this stuff guys. You can end up with all this garbage."

Key stat: "25% of all systems we install are actually cheap, rubbish systems that people got scammed by. One in four systems on people's roofs we have to rip off because they've been scammed by another company. This is serious. We don't want you to be one of those."

"A quality solar system pays for itself. A cheap one ends up in landfill. Yet thousands of homeowners still make the same mistake - they buy it twice. Cheap first, then properly second."

Long-term thinker pre-frame:
"Unfortunately guys, the average person just struggles to think long-term. It's a sad thing. They see a system that's a little bit cheaper and think, oh yep, I'll grab that one. Or they genuinely can't afford it. Or they get tricked by one of those dodgy companies. It just sucks to see so many people get ripped off so badly. Honestly, just find yourself a company that actually cares about you, that's going to take care of you long-term. As long as they're not unfairly priced - it still works out less than what you're already paying on power anyways. Why would you even risk going cheap?"

Note the framing: NOT "why would you go with anyone else except us" (sales pressure). It's "why would you even risk going cheap?" - you care about them.

BEAT 8 - 25-Year SLA Warranty:

"Actually guys, I bring this up because it is quite relevant to one of our warranties I really wanted to tell you about."

"We have something put in place - one of our warranties that I think is going to genuinely resonate with you. It's our 25-year service level agreement. What that means is for the full 25 years that your system is on the roof - that's your solar, your battery, your inverter, the cables, the switches, the racking, every single part of the system - we actually guarantee it will never go down for the full 25 years. And if it ever does, we'll come out to fix it within X days."

[X = whatever your company can actually facilitate]

"The reason we do that - we don't want your solar breaking down every couple of years, problems with the inverter, battery, bills creeping back in. We just want you to know: you've put it up, you've done it right, you've picked a quality company, there's going to be no problems for 25 years. That's the whole point of this warranty - to protect you and make sure we put your mind at ease always. Does that make sense?"

EXECUTION NOTES:
- Monitor their facial reactions throughout this entire pillar
- If energy drops and they're zoning out - ask a quick question to spike it back up
- Never beat a slide harder if you can tell they're bored
- If you're getting "can you move on" reactions a lot, it means you're not executing with enough certainty and genuine emotional conviction
- This is the most content-heavy pillar - you must look engaged, passionate, and genuinely concerned
- Script this out and use exact scripting provided - this pillar directly determines whether you get objections at the end
