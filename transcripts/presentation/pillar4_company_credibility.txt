PILLAR 4: Company Credibility
Source: Presentation Modules

Coming straight off Pillar 3 (industry problems), Pillar 4 shows them: we are the solution to the problem we just painted. Tonality: relaxed, confident, matter-of-fact. You're stating facts, not being salesy.

GOAL: By the end of this pillar, they think: "This is a good company. This is a legitimate company. This is a company I would feel comfortable being in a 25-year relationship with."

From this point onwards it should be smooth sailing - they won't be pushing you to jump to the end anymore because from here it's all the stuff they've been wanting to see (products, design, savings, calculations, warranties).

READING THE ROOM:
They've just come through 3-4 very heavy slides. If you can see they're zoning out - don't push through all the text. Bang, bring it straight to the surf clubs, re-engage them:
"Guys, do you get to the beach much?" - they're back in it.
If they're still engaged, read through and reinforce it all.

BEAT 1 - The Surf Club Standard:

Re-engage question first: "By the way guys, do you get to the beach much? How far are you from your local beach?"
Ask a couple questions, get them speaking, build rapport. Then:

"This is something I think you guys are gonna find really cool - it's one of our biggest commitments to you."

"Guys, when you see the panels and all the equipment up on the roofs - what's the first thing you're thinking of when you see it in this exact environment?" [highlight the ocean]

They'll say something about the ocean/salt corrosion - you answer for them:
"Yeah, exactly - the ocean, the salt water, all of that."

"When we first started doing installs, we looked at what was the harshest possible condition, and we decided - let's replicate that exactly - to make sure that our customers have that complete assurance that wherever they're located, the quality of their equipment is actually proven in the harshest possible environments. So on your property, it'll work no problems for 25 years."

Touch on:
- Harshest environment: right on the beachfront, heavy winds, heavy sand
- "You know what happens when you've got salt with metal - rust straight away. If you've ever been near the beach, you know what happens with the rust."
- Council approval process: "The council actually had to approve before anything went on the Surf Clubs. It had to go through 15 levels of council approval. Every single nut, bolt, screw was scrutinised, reviewed several times. Finally it got allowed. So if the council did all of that due diligence and hard work and background checks, you can feel pretty confident that what we're using is pretty good stuff."

The Surf Club Guarantee (say this directly):
"Here's our commitment to you guys. We call it our Surf Club Standard. We guarantee the same quality of products and standards that we used on the Surf Clubs is what we put on every single install we do - including yours. Because the way we see it, if it survives the Surf Club, it'll survive your home for 25 years, no problem."

NOTE: This is only doable if you use Longi panels. Strongly recommended - it's what the presentation is built on, what Resync used, and they're the best panels. If you use different panels, adjust accordingly.

BEAT 2 - Credentials Slide:

Plug in your company's numbers: years in business, total installations, total savings for customers, awards, accreditations.

If you've only been in business 1 year - find other metrics that show you in a good light.

Subbies warning (explain this to them):
"A lot of these companies will just use subbies. If they ever tell you they don't have their own electricians - guys, run the other way. Because if something goes wrong, who's going to get held accountable? The subbies point the finger at the company. The company points the finger at the subbies. And guess who at the end of it gets no resolution? You guys."

Use your accreditations to your advantage - explain what each one means. Whatever accreditations your company has, use them. The whole goal: they see you as a credible company.

BEAT 3 - Reviews (extremely intentional):

Reviews must be chosen to pre-handle specific objections. Don't just pick any good review.

Example Review 1 (handles: "will they care about me after install?"):
"We had a system installed back in 2020, and honestly, the thing that's stood out the most isn't the panels. It's the people. Five years on, they still call to check in, still help me understand my bills, still pick up the phone when I have a question. I've had appliances I bought last year with worse customer service. Couldn't recommend more."

Example Review 2 (handles: "will it actually lower my bills?"):
"Before solar, our power bills were brutal - over $800 a quarter. Now $42. The company still comes around. The savings haven't dropped. The best money we've spent on the house."

Every review should directly pre-handle a specific concern: post-install support, bill savings, company reliability.

BEAT 4 - Handling Bad Reviews Pre-emptively:

Always finish the reviews section with this (word for word):
"Guys, to be completely honest with you - have we had bad reviews before? Listen, of course we have. You know, we've all got an iPhone in our pocket. The reality is, we're all human beings. Something could potentially happen on the day of install. Maybe even one in a thousand. And someone gets a bit emotional about a bit of a mess, or they can't use the app properly. They take a quick snap, put up a review. It's happened a couple of times guys. You can probably find a couple poor reviews out of the X thousand reviews we've received."

"But listen - the number one way you can judge a company is what happens when things go wrong. That's the real test."

Then add a personal founder story:
"Just two weekends ago, our founder [name] spent 1.5 hours on a Saturday on the phone with a customer trying to resolve an issue. That's just the kind of company this is. And that's why I love working here."

Reference a review that shows: issue happened + company immediately resolved it. That is the most powerful type of review.

- Don't rush - if they're engaged, go through everything
- If they're zoning out, re-engage with a question before continuing
- Script this out yourself using exact scripting provided
