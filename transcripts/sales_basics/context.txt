CONTEXTUALISING YOUR QUESTIONS
Source: Sales Basics Module

Asking a question on its own often lands wrong. Adding context - either from what they've said or a brief setup - makes questions feel natural, reasonable, and gets better answers.

THE PROBLEM WITH BARE QUESTIONS:
"After 10 years, if the bills are at X amount - how are you going to deal with that?"
Potentially builds resistance. Feels like a challenge. Could come across as pushy.

WITH CONTEXT:
"And listen [name] - my best intention here is to help. So if 10 years from now the bills are at X amount - how are you going to deal with that?"
Now they think: he's asking because he cares, not because he's trying to trap me. Same question, completely different landing.

CLOSING CONTEXT EXAMPLE:
Bad: "What decision should you make right now?"

With context built from their stated goals:
"Understanding that 5-10 years from now we want to be in a position where we're looking back, paying nothing on power, we've got a $50,000 asset on our home, we're happy with the decision we made, we're better off financially - understanding we want to put ourselves in that situation... what do you feel like is the best decision to make now in light of that?"

What happened: you used their own desired outcome as the context for the question. It feels collaborative, not pressured. And the answer almost writes itself.

RAPPORT CONTEXT EXAMPLE:
They mention they have 5 kids.
Bad: "How do you have time to work during the day?"
With context: "Understanding you've got 5 kids - how do you still have time to work during the day?"
The first lands weird. The second is natural and shows you were listening.

THE PRINCIPLE:
Collect context throughout the entire conversation. Drop it back into your questions. It makes conversations feel authentic instead of scripted, and it signals that you've been listening the whole time.

Two sources of context to use:
1. What they've told you (their life, their goals, their concerns)
2. A brief setup that explains your intention ("my best intention is to help", "just so I understand your situation better")

Start doing this and questions will land better immediately.
