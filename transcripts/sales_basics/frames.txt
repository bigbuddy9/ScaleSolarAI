FRAMES
Source: Sales Basics Module

A frame is putting a lens over something before you say it. It primes the listener to receive what's coming with the right level of attention and the right emotional state.

THE CONCEPT:
Without a frame: you just say the thing.
With a frame: you set up why they should care before you say the thing.

EXAMPLES:

Jumping straight in (no frame):
"So we have the Surf Club Standard..." [they're half-listening]

With a frame:
"Guys, what I'm about to walk you through is literally one of the coolest things you'll hear about our company." → then explain the Surf Club Standard.
Result: they lean in. They're already curious. "What is so cool?"

Linking to a warranty:
"What I'm about to tell you actually links directly to one of our warranties - and personally I think it will save you thousands of dollars." → then go into it.
Result: they're already engaged before you've said anything of substance.

Asking a personal question:
Instead of just asking: "How much is your power bill?"
Frame it: "Just so I can understand your situation better..." → then ask the question.
Result: the question feels reasonable and natural. They give a more honest answer.

WHY FRAMES WORK:
- They create anticipation and buy-in before the content lands
- They make questions feel logical and reasonable to answer
- They make the customer feel like they're about to hear something valuable, not be pitched

HOW TO GET BETTER:
- Practice using frames in day-to-day conversation
- In the script, frames are marked where to use them - follow those
- Start noticing when you're jumping straight into things and ask: could I frame this first?

Simple concept. High impact. Once it becomes a habit you'll see it everywhere and use it instinctively.
