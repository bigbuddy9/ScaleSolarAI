OBJECTION HANDLING
Source: Sales Basics Module

Stay in rapport no matter what. Everything else is secondary. If you lose rapport, you have 0% chance of the sale and they'll ignore your follow-ups.

The more times you ask for the sale (while staying in rapport), the higher your close rate. Closing 5 times beats closing once. Never stop at the first no.

---

OBJECTION 1: "IT'S TOO EXPENSIVE"

First response - Curious tonality:
"Interesting. Depends on how you view it though."
[Let them think: what do you mean?]

"The people who end up the wealthiest aren't the ones who look at something and straight away go, 'way too expensive.' That's actually how you miss out on opportunities. How you stay stuck paying the power companies stupid amounts of money forever. The wealthy ones don't react to the price tag and immediately shut it down. They take a second to understand the investment, assess whether it's a smart long-term move, look at whether it gives a good return, make sure it's safeguarded with no risk on their end. All those boxes are ticked here. The only question is whether you keep paying the power companies forever, or you start paying into something you actually own - something that's paying you back. That should be obvious."

[Let them respond]

If they push back again - "when you say too much, are you comparing it to what you're currently paying on power, or is it just more than you want to spend per month?":
"Here's the thing. Your power bill is X per month right now. That's not guaranteed to stay there. It's gone up every year. It'll keep going up. The system, on the other hand, is X per month - locked in, doesn't spike, doesn't move. So you're either going to keep paying a number that climbs every year into something you'll never own, or you redirect that same money into an asset sitting on your home that's actually paying you back. Same money leaving your account either way. The only difference is where it lands and what you've got to show for it at the end."

[Curious - wait for their response]

If still pushing:
"Look - I'm not here to talk you into something you're not comfortable with. If price is genuinely the concern, I'd rather know now. How could we make this number a little bit more digestible for you?"
[Frame it as: we're here to help. We want to find a way.]

---

OBJECTION 2: "I NEED TO THINK ABOUT IT"

Never say: "No, no, you can't." That raises resistance immediately.

First: bring it down.
"Yeah, of course. No stress."

Then:
"Just so I understand - how do you feel about the fit? Like, do you like [company name]? Do you feel like we'd have your back and look after you guys long term?"
[They say yes]
"Oh, why do you say that?"
[Get them to elaborate - they're now building the case in their own mind for why your company is the right one]

Then:
"Right, so the fit's right, you're happy with the system. Listen - my best intention here is to help. What's actually making you think it's not sensible to lock this in today?"
[Go silent. Wait. This extracts the REAL objection.]

"Think about it" is almost always a mask. Find what's underneath it.

THE CERTAINTY HANDLE:
"Fair enough. And honestly, that's a smart way to be - people who make rash decisions usually get burnt."
"Let me ask you - do you think maybe that comes from not being 100% certain in the investment? Like, because if you were 100% certain, you'd obviously do it."
[They agree]
"But here's the thing - can you ever be 100% certain about anything? Like, even if I went to Harvard, studied my whole life, got the degree - can I be 100% certain I'll get the job I want? No. You can never be 100% certain about anything."
[Get them to agree]
"Same here. You could keep thinking about this for weeks, months, and still wouldn't know 100% if it's the right move. Nobody ever does on anything that matters. But what I do know is - 3, 6, 12 months from now, when you're looking back and your bills are in credit, you're going to say: thank God we did that. To be in that situation, what decision do you have to make right now?"
[Soft close]

---

OBJECTION 3: "I WANT TO SHOP AROUND / GET OTHER QUOTES"

This usually means Pillar 3 wasn't executed hard enough. Handle it.

Same opening:
"Yeah, of course, that makes sense."
"Just so I understand - how do you feel about the fit?"
[Same as above]
"Right - so if the fit's right and you're happy with the system, my best intention here is to help. What's actually making you think it's not sensible to lock this in today instead of going and looking around?"
[Wait for the real objection. Address it. Then go to the certainty handle.]

"You could keep looking around for the rest of the time - but the truth is, you keep wasting money by waiting. Every month is another month you've paid the power company instead of owning something."

---

BOOKING THE HIGH-INTENT FOLLOW-UP (when nothing is closing them right now):

"Yeah, I completely appreciate that. To be honest, I'm pretty similar myself - I don't like making big decisions on the spot either. We're pretty under the pump at the moment with the current [rebate/offer]. So let me squeeze in a quick 30 minutes either tomorrow or the day after. I'll go over everything in detail, answer anything that's come up for you over the next couple of days - just to help you guys out. If that sounds fair, just let me know which day works better."

Tie it to urgency: the offer has to be discussed in the next 24-48 hours because of XYZ limited thing.

---

BONUS REFRAMES (use when fitting, not every time):

1-TO-10 CALIBRATION:
"On a scale of 1 to 10 - 1 being this is the worst investment you've ever seen, 10 being great investment, great ROI, no risk - where are you guys right now?"
[They give a number - usually 6-8]
"What would make it a 10?"
[They tell you the real concern. Address it.]
"Cool - so now are you feeling more like a 10?"
[Loop until resolved or it's clear they need follow-up]

If low number: "That's lower than I'd expect. What's the main thing pulling that number down?"

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS A NO:
"Fair enough. Let me flip it on you then - what would make this a no for you guys? Because from what you've said, the fit's right, the system's right, the partnership's right. So if you walked away from this and told your mate - 'I didn't go with them' - what's the reason you give?"
[Gets them to state the honest truth. Address it.]

HOW MUCH HAS NOT DECIDING ALREADY COST YOU:
"Honestly - can I ask you something? How much has not deciding on this already cost you up to this point? Because your bills have been climbing for years. Every month you've been thinking 'maybe one day' - that's another month you've handed to the power company instead of into something you own. That's already cost you thousands, probably tens of thousands by now. I'm not saying that to make you feel bad. But I think the real question isn't whether this costs you much - it's whether you can afford another year, two years, five years of the same delay that's already cost you so much."
[Concerned tone throughout. Genuine. Not forceful.]

SAME MISTAKE THAT GOT YOU HERE:
"I hear you. And I don't want you to feel any pressure. But can I ask - you mentioned you've been looking at solar for [X years]. Do you think maybe the reason you're still in this exact situation - still paying the power companies a fortune, still getting smashed by these rates - is because of that pattern of putting the decision off? Because if that pattern is what got you here, do you reckon making the same call again is going to get you somewhere different? I just don't want you to look back in another two years wishing you'd done it now."
[Adjust vocabulary based on who you're speaking to]

THE BURNING HOUSE ANALOGY (use sparingly, when you want to show you genuinely care):
"Can I ask you something - have you heard of the burning house analogy? If you saw someone trapped inside a burning house, you wouldn't politely respect their comfort. You wouldn't say 'take your time.' You wouldn't walk away because you didn't want to be annoying. Because when someone is in a bad situation, urgency isn't manipulation - it's responsibility. If I saw a friend getting ripped off, paying way more than they needed to for something, and I saw a much better solution - I'd want them to know. That's why I do this. Not because I'm trying to push you into anything - because I genuinely believe what we're showing you here is a better situation that would actually help you."
[Raw, honest, sincere. Very intentional tonality throughout.]

---

CORE PRINCIPLE:
Come from a place of genuinely wanting to help them. Every reframe, every close, every objection handle - it should be executed as: I care about you, I can see this is going to benefit you, and I want to make sure you don't miss out on something that would genuinely improve your life. That energy is what makes objection handling land.

Master this and you'll add 10-15%+ to your close rate immediately.
