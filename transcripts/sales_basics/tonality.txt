TONALITY
Source: Sales Basics Module

7% of what you say is communicated through your words. 93% is tonality and body language. Master tonality and you can dictate the emotion the prospect feels at any point in the call.

Without tonality, you cannot dictate outcomes. With it, you can guide every conversation exactly where you want it to go.

THE 12 TONALITIES:

1. CURIOUS (childlike curiosity)
Use when: asking questions that could get resistance, or that need a genuine honest answer.
"How come you guys haven't gotten solar yet?" [uptone at the end, genuine interest]
"How are you doing today?" [actually interested, not robotic]
Think: a child asking something - they genuinely want to know. Uptone at the end signals real curiosity.

2. CONFUSED / SKEPTICAL
Use when: you want the prospect to feel compelled to clarify or engage.
"Hey, are you guys... are you guys the homeowners here?" [hesitant, slightly puzzled]
Result: They instinctively want to address the confusion. Creates engagement immediately.
Contrast: saying it flatly gets you dismissed. Saying it confused gets them leaning in.

3. CASUAL (the default tonality)
Use: as your baseline. The opposite of every salesperson in history.
"I've just got an appointment in a couple of minutes, literally just down the road."
"I'm just Jesse from XYZ company."
Casual = talking to a friend. Never formal, never stiff. Keep everything calm and natural.
This is what makes people think: he's not a salesperson. He's just a person.

4. CONCERNED
Use when: you want them to feel you genuinely care about their situation.
"Wow, you're getting smashed." [drop your volume, soften your tone]
Think: how you'd ask a friend "are you okay?" - that's the exact energy.
Shows you care. Builds rapport. Makes them feel looked after.

5. CERTAIN
Use when: stating facts about your product or offer.
"You can actually put a system on the roof for literally zero cost." [conviction, no wavering]
Not flat. Not over the top. Just stated with absolute confidence.
Certainty is contagious. If you believe it, they believe it.

6. INTRIGUE / MYSTERY
Use when: you want to pique their interest and get buy-in.
"There is a genuine reason I'm here... I'll show you." [slow down, fluctuate tone, almost like you're thinking]
Result: "What? What's the reason? I'm interested now."
Slow your words. Act like what you're about to say is significant. Because it is.
Don't rush through it - the pause and the build is the whole point.

7. REASONABLE
Use when: presenting the finance/redirect concept as obvious common sense.
"Yeah, you can just redirect it to pay off the system." [shrug, matter-of-fact]
Think: body language of a casual shrug. "It's just that simple. No big deal."
"Does that sound fair enough?" → "Yeah, that sounds fair enough." That's what you want.

8. OBVIOUS
Use when: making something feel like plain common sense without saying "obviously."
"Yeah, I mean... who's opposed to saving a hundred bucks off their bill?" [light, self-evident]
Not debating it. Not trying to convince. Just stating something so clear it doesn't need to be argued.
Avoid saying the word "obviously" - it's overused and lands badly. Use the tonality instead.

9. GENUINE / SINCERE
Use when: making statements that need to land as authentic.
"I'm not here to sell you anything today." [lower voice, lean in, genuine]
This has to feel real. If your whole energy up to this point has been casual and non-salesy, this lands perfectly. If not, it sounds scripted.
Sincerity = quieter, slower, more personal.

10. FAMILIAR
Use when: referencing neighbours, shared context, creating connection.
"I just finished helping out a neighbour a couple blocks over." [warm, like an old friend]
Think: the energy of running into someone you know. Warm, easy, natural.
Makes you sound like part of the community, not a stranger pitching.

11. PLAYFUL
Use when: lightening tension, making something sound low-stakes.
"It's not like you've signed into some 25-year contract or something crazy." [smile, chuckle]
Smile while you say it. Chuckle a little. Keep it light.
Playfulness dissolves resistance better than any logical argument.

12. SCARCITY / URGENCY
Use when: closing, referencing the case study offer, government rebate timing.
"There's only a couple left... but you'd probably be able to get one." [slow down, lower volume]
Think: Wolf of Wall Street - how DiCaprio delivers urgency on the phone. That controlled intensity.
"This just came across my desk. It's limited."
Quiet, deliberate, measured. Not frantic - controlled urgency is more powerful.

HOW TO GET BETTER:
- Practice all 12 daily
- Catch them in real life - movies, conversations, podcasts
- When you hear someone speaking, identify the tonality and what outcome they're going for
- Once you start thinking this way, you'll have dramatically more control on sales calls

The goal: every tonality is intentional. Nothing is accidental. You know exactly what response you're going for before you open your mouth.
